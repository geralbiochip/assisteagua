How to calculate BTUs for air conditioning?

Choosing the right air conditioning capacity is important for thermal comfort, energy efficiency and performance. An undersized system may not condition the room adequately, while an oversized unit may increase electricity consumption unnecessarily.

Our BTU Calculator estimates the capacity recommended for each room, considering area, sun exposure, windows, occupancy and thermal insulation.

What does BTU mean?

BTU (British Thermal Unit) is the unit used to express cooling and heating capacity. A higher BTU rating indicates greater capacity to cool or heat a space.

Choosing the right BTU rating helps achieve comfort and energy efficiency throughout the year.

Why choose the right capacity?

Many people choose air conditioning by price or brand alone. Suitable capacity is equally important.

An undersized unit may run continuously without reaching the desired temperature. An oversized unit may increase energy consumption and reduce efficiency.

We therefore recommend estimating the BTUs required before choosing a system.

How to calculate BTUs per m²?

A common question is how many BTUs are needed to condition a room correctly.

Capacity depends primarily on the usable floor area.

Besides area, other factors can influence the appropriate capacity:

  • number of people in the room;
  • number of windows;
  • sun exposure;
  • thermal insulation;
  • electronic equipment;
  • room orientation.

Our BTU Calculator considers these factors to provide a quick, practical estimate.

How many BTUs does an apartment need?

In apartments, the capacity required depends on room size and how each space is used.

As a general guide:

  • Bedrooms up to 15 m² usually need between 5,000 and 7,000 BTU.
  • Rooms between 20 and 30 m² commonly need approximately 9,000 BTU.
  • Living rooms of around 35 m² may require 12,000 BTU.
  • Large living rooms or open-plan spaces may need 18,000 BTU or more.

Direct sunlight and the number of windows can significantly increase heat load. If in doubt, we recommend a professional consultation and design assessment.

BTUs for bedrooms

Bedrooms generally have a lower heat load. In many cases, a unit between 5,000 and 9,000 BTU can provide year-round comfort.

BTUs for living rooms

Living rooms often accommodate more people and electronic equipment and receive more sunlight. They generally require higher-capacity units.

Depending on area and room characteristics, recommended capacity may range from 12,000 to 18,000 BTU.

BTUs for offices

Computers, monitors, lighting and continuous occupancy often add to an office’s heat load. Careful sizing helps provide a comfortable, productive environment.

FAQ · Frequently asked questions

How many BTUs do I need for 25 m²?

For approximately 25 m², recommended capacity is usually between 9,000 and 12,000 BTU, depending on sunlight, windows and occupancy.
